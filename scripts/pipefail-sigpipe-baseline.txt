# WHY HIGH IS THE ENFORCED TIER. `high` is the scanner's own label for a producer
# that is unbounded or file-sized — `find`/`git`/`docker`/`ls`/a full `grep` into
# `grep -q`/`head`. There the 141 is not a maybe: the producer will outrun a
# 64KB pipe as soon as the tree it reads grows. `medium` (136) is the shape of
# the live bug — `printf "$var" | grep -q` — but its variables are usually short
# today, so a gate at medium reds on 136 pre-existing sites on its first PR, and
# a gate that reds on every PR from day one gets switched off rather than
# obeyed. medium and low are therefore INVENTORY, printed and not enforced.
#
# WHY A RATCHET AND NOT A CEILING. The count may FALL freely; it may never RISE.
# A rise is a NEW site, and the whole point is that this class is latent — it
# ships green and reds months later on an unrelated PR. Note that the scanner
# does NOT red when the number falls (it prints RATCHET LOOSE): a check that
# fails when the world gets better teaches its readers to regenerate the number,
# and regeneration is where a real regression gets laundered in.
#
# TO LOWER IT: fix a site, re-run the command above, put its printed count here,
# and update the date and composition in this header in the same commit.
# TO RAISE IT: don't. A rise means a new site was added; fix the site.
