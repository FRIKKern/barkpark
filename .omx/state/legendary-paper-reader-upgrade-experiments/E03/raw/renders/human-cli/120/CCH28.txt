THE FINDING: wave 27 named the shallow clone in its own fix's header comment, and did not close it

seal-predicate.mjs:453 is one line: `try { execFileSync('git', ['-C', REPO, 'merge-base', '--is-ancestor', commit,
'origin/main'], …); } catch { problems.push(`commit ${commit} is not an ancestor of origin/main`); }`. The catch is
unconditional. Charter D318 enumerated exactly what it swallows — “not-a-git-repo / no-origin-main / shallow-clone /
unknown-sha” — and wave 27's fix closed the first two and wrote the other two into a comment.

The wave-27 header comment says it out loud, verbatim: “the ancestry `catch` at the top of `verifyCommit` swallows not-
a-git-repo, no origin/main, a shallow clone and an unknown sha into one identical claim about the PRODUCT.” The remedy
that follows checks that `.github/workflows/cloud.yml` exists and that `git rev-parse --show-toplevel` resolves to REPO.
A depth-1 CI clone satisfies both.

THE DIRECTION: one disease, two audiences — the console and its instrument both stop asserting what they did not measure

This wave has one sentence and it points in both directions at once. A system that could not determine something must
say so. It must not substitute a plausible guess, a default, or the negative branch of a two-way test it was unable to
run.

Pointed at the INSTRUMENT, that sentence resolves the lead's binary by refusing it. Option (a) alone leaves the
predicate lying in every other shallow environment — a container, a fork PR, an agent's `--depth 1`, any future job.
Option (b) alone leaves standing the fact the lead correctly identified as bigger than the test: that every seal run
under this workflow is structurally unable to evaluate clause (b). The answer that pays both is the DISCRIMINATION:
teach the git legs to tell rc 1 (“not an ancestor”, an honest answer about the product) from rc 128 / missing object /
shallow repository (“I could not look”, an honest answer about the environment). Once the predicate can tell those
apart, (b) becomes the correct shape for the control — it asserts the floor it is actually about — and (a) becomes a
verified environment declaration rather than a workaround, because a regression back to depth-1 would then produce an
honest INFRA-FAULT the job can assert on.

Pointed at the PERSON, the same sentence is already sitting in the ledger as open rows. A refused connection is reported
as “A network step timed out. Retry usually fixes this.” — the console did not classify the failure, so it guessed, and
the guess carries ADVICE. A person switches on “Deployment failed” alerts across email and every chat channel, and the
event has zero producers anywhere in the repository — the console tells them they are subscribed to something that
cannot arrive. These are not clipping bugs. They are the epic's founding vision, in its purest form, and they are the
wave's person bodies.

The direction debate: three candidates, argued, one committed

Rival A — “Finish the instrument, build the door” (the wish read literally)

Take the lead's binary, pick one leg, and spend the rest of the wave on the D307 door guard as a full multi-slice build:
the `surface` enum, the backfill, the fence ruling, the refusal, the mutation proof. Clean, obedient, and it retires the
highest-leverage row in the epic.

WHY IT LOSES. Three counts. First, arithmetic: four instrument slices against a floor of four person bodies means eight
slices minimum, and this epic's own history says a wave that spends 4-of-6 on instruments moves the row count the wrong
way (waves 9/10/11: +11, +16, +7). Second, the door guard's dependency chain — enum → backfill → fence ruling → refusal
— is SERIAL and cannot fan out to parallel builders, so it converts a fan-out wave into a queue. Third and worst, it
accepts the false binary. Picking (a) or (b) at speed leaves the predicate lying in every shallow environment that is
not this one workflow, and this Paper has now proved live that such an environment exists and that the epic's own
instrument is in it.

Rival B — “Pay Law 0 to zero and drive at the seal”

orphans is 57 and a=FAIL is the only clause failing. Spend the wave closing, forwarding and adjudicating residue —
including the cch-w22-s7 split D325 already ruled — and get the epic within sight of an honest SEAL.

WHY IT LOSES. It is the epic's stated disease wearing a productivity costume. The charter's vision is that the CONSOLE
stops lying; a wave with zero person bodies fails the ratio outright and would be the fourth restatement of a law rather
than a payment of it. It also mistakes the seal for the goal — D318 proved the predicate could SEAL an epic that does
not exist, which means a falling orphan count was never the thing worth optimising. HARVESTED ANYWAY: this Paper quotes
the reading with all three provenance facts, and the cch-w22-s7 adjudication is booked as a Decide-phase ledger action
for the lead, costing zero builders.

Rival C — “One disease, two audiences” — COMMITTED

Two instrument slices that pay the environment question properly plus the discrimination that generalises it; one door-
guard slice made buildable by moving its backfill dependency onto the lead's hands at Decide; and four-to-five person
bodies chosen so that at least two of them are literally the same defect the instrument has — a system asserting a fact
it did not measure.

WHY IT WINS. It is the only candidate where the instrument work and the person work are the SAME argument, which means
the wave's finding transfers: the sentence that fixes the seal predicate is the sentence a builder applies to
`connection refused`. It keeps the ratio at four-plus without inventing filler. It refuses the lead's binary on evidence
rather than on preference — the depth-1 run is measured, not reasoned. And it ships the door guard ARMED this wave by
removing the dependency that blocked it, rather than ruling on it for a third consecutive wave.

What is now ESTABLISHED

1. The seal predicate's two git legs disagree about the same failure class, four lines apart

On origin/main, seal-predicate.mjs:453-454 catches ANY throw from merge-base --is-ancestor and pushes a definite product
claim ("commit X is not an ancestor of origin/main"). :457-458 catches the identical class from git show and throws

far stronger charter sentence than "add discrimination to the git legs", and it explains why the lead's stubbing remedy
failed: stubbing :453 hands control to :457, which correctly raises Infra. The lead patched the leg that was already
right.

2. The discrimination the direction proposed is INSUFFICIENT — proved live, and this is the survey's crown

The direction proposed keying on rc 1 (product) vs rc 128 / missing object / shallow (environment). A surveyor built
four real clones and measured the truth table. The residual case breaks it: in a --depth 1 clone with the commit object
fetched IN (grafted), cat-file -e returns rc 0, git show --name-only returns rc 0 with real bytes — and merge-base --is-

2. `:nxdomain` IS AN ANSWER, NOT A FAULT (commits 90ab9999a + 438495a96) — a REVIEW DECISION superseding the letter of
D332. The s7 builder built D332 as written, measured that one fifth of it was wrong, and filed task-3fbfff8c97b50c8f at

nxdomain for a missing record instead of `{:ok, []}`, a shape production never produces. task-3fbfff8c97b50c8f is closed
with the ruling, its measurement and its residual gap on the record.
