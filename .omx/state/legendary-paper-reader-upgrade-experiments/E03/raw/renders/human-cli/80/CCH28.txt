THE FINDING: wave 27 named the shallow clone in its own fix's header comment,
and did not close it

seal-predicate.mjs:453 is one line: `try { execFileSync('git', ['-C', REPO,
'merge-base', '--is-ancestor', commit, 'origin/main'], …); } catch {
problems.push(`commit ${commit} is not an ancestor of origin/main`); }`. The
catch is unconditional. Charter D318 enumerated exactly what it swallows — “not-
a-git-repo / no-origin-main / shallow-clone / unknown-sha” — and wave 27's fix
closed the first two and wrote the other two into a comment.

The wave-27 header comment says it out loud, verbatim: “the ancestry `catch` at
the top of `verifyCommit` swallows not-a-git-repo, no origin/main, a shallow
clone and an unknown sha into one identical claim about the PRODUCT.” The remedy
that follows checks that `.github/workflows/cloud.yml` exists and that `git rev-
parse --show-toplevel` resolves to REPO. A depth-1 CI clone satisfies both.

THE DIRECTION: one disease, two audiences — the console and its instrument both
stop asserting what they did not measure

This wave has one sentence and it points in both directions at once. A system
that could not determine something must say so. It must not substitute a
plausible guess, a default, or the negative branch of a two-way test it was
unable to run.

Pointed at the INSTRUMENT, that sentence resolves the lead's binary by refusing
it. Option (a) alone leaves the predicate lying in every other shallow
environment — a container, a fork PR, an agent's `--depth 1`, any future job.
Option (b) alone leaves standing the fact the lead correctly identified as
bigger than the test: that every seal run under this workflow is structurally
unable to evaluate clause (b). The answer that pays both is the DISCRIMINATION:
teach the git legs to tell rc 1 (“not an ancestor”, an honest answer about the
product) from rc 128 / missing object / shallow repository (“I could not look”,
an honest answer about the environment). Once the predicate can tell those
apart, (b) becomes the correct shape for the control — it asserts the floor it
is actually about — and (a) becomes a verified environment declaration rather
than a workaround, because a regression back to depth-1 would then produce an
honest INFRA-FAULT the job can assert on.

Pointed at the PERSON, the same sentence is already sitting in the ledger as
open rows. A refused connection is reported as “A network step timed out. Retry
usually fixes this.” — the console did not classify the failure, so it guessed,
and the guess carries ADVICE. A person switches on “Deployment failed” alerts
across email and every chat channel, and the event has zero producers anywhere
in the repository — the console tells them they are subscribed to something that
cannot arrive. These are not clipping bugs. They are the epic's founding vision,
in its purest form, and they are the wave's person bodies.

What the finished wave looks like

  restructured to assert clause (a) — the floor it exists to test — not because
  anything red was weakened. Nothing about the depth-1 environment changed to
  get it green.

  two: HISTORY-UNAVAILABLE. Run it in a shallow clone and it exits 2 with a
  named code and zero invented findings, exactly as it now does in a git-archive
  extraction. Mutation-proved with a REAL `git clone --depth 1`, not a stub —
  the wave-27 lesson is that stubbing the leg only moves the failure.
• console-harness.yml stops being an environment the instrument cannot work in.
  The seal job declares the history it needs, and a self-check reds if that
  declaration silently reverts — a guard on the guard's environment, which is
  the class of defect this whole wave is about.
• The D307 door guard is ARMED, not ruled on for a third wave. `surface` becomes
  an enum; the open rows are backfilled BY THE LEAD'S HAND at Decide (wave 27

  not the whole task system.

The direction debate: three candidates, argued, one committed

Rival A — “Finish the instrument, build the door” (the wish read literally)

Take the lead's binary, pick one leg, and spend the rest of the wave on the D307
door guard as a full multi-slice build: the `surface` enum, the backfill, the
fence ruling, the refusal, the mutation proof. Clean, obedient, and it retires
the highest-leverage row in the epic.

WHY IT LOSES. Three counts. First, arithmetic: four instrument slices against a
floor of four person bodies means eight slices minimum, and this epic's own
history says a wave that spends 4-of-6 on instruments moves the row count the
wrong way (waves 9/10/11: +11, +16, +7). Second, the door guard's dependency
chain — enum → backfill → fence ruling → refusal — is SERIAL and cannot fan out
to parallel builders, so it converts a fan-out wave into a queue. Third and
worst, it accepts the false binary. Picking (a) or (b) at speed leaves the
predicate lying in every shallow environment that is not this one workflow, and
this Paper has now proved live that such an environment exists and that the
epic's own instrument is in it.

Rival B — “Pay Law 0 to zero and drive at the seal”

orphans is 57 and a=FAIL is the only clause failing. Spend the wave closing,
forwarding and adjudicating residue — including the cch-w22-s7 split D325
already ruled — and get the epic within sight of an honest SEAL.

WHY IT LOSES. It is the epic's stated disease wearing a productivity costume.
The charter's vision is that the CONSOLE stops lying; a wave with zero person
bodies fails the ratio outright and would be the fourth restatement of a law
rather than a payment of it. It also mistakes the seal for the goal — D318
proved the predicate could SEAL an epic that does not exist, which means a
falling orphan count was never the thing worth optimising. HARVESTED ANYWAY:
this Paper quotes the reading with all three provenance facts, and the cch-w22-
s7 adjudication is booked as a Decide-phase ledger action for the lead, costing
zero builders.

Rival C — “One disease, two audiences” — COMMITTED

Two instrument slices that pay the environment question properly plus the
discrimination that generalises it; one door-guard slice made buildable by
moving its backfill dependency onto the lead's hands at Decide; and four-to-five
person bodies chosen so that at least two of them are literally the same defect
the instrument has — a system asserting a fact it did not measure.

WHY IT WINS. It is the only candidate where the instrument work and the person
work are the SAME argument, which means the wave's finding transfers: the
sentence that fixes the seal predicate is the sentence a builder applies to
`connection refused`. It keeps the ratio at four-plus without inventing filler.
It refuses the lead's binary on evidence rather than on preference — the depth-1
run is measured, not reasoned. And it ships the door guard ARMED this wave by
removing the dependency that blocked it, rather than ruling on it for a third
consecutive wave.

The sharpest attack on the winner, and how the wave absorbs it

▌ “You are deferring the D307 door guard AGAIN. Wave 26 ruled on it, wave 27
▌ filed it, and ‘build the enum, defer the refusal’ is exactly the shape that
▌ lets wave 29 defer it a fourth time. Meanwhile you have talked yourself into
▌ a THIRD instrument slice nobody asked for.”

TAKEN, and it changes the plan rather than being argued away. The wave does not
ship the enum and defer the refusal — it ships the refusal ARMED, and it removes
the blocker by re-classifying it. D324 gave four honest reasons the guard could
not ship: (i) no classifier, (ii) the fence widening is not a builder's call,
(iii) CI is post-hoc detection not refusal, (iv) shipping before the backfill
makes the guard's first act on main a refusal of legitimate filings. Reasons (i)
and (iv) are LEDGER work, and wave 27 proved conclusively that ledger work
belongs to the lead's hands at Decide — 57 re-parents, executed in minutes, with
the post-condition asserted on the destination roster. Reason (ii) is a numbered
charter decision, which is also a Decide-phase artifact. Reason (iii) argues FOR
the door, not against it. So the builder's slice is what remains: the enum
validation and the refusal, scoped to one parent_id, with a mutation proof that
shows it can both refuse and pass. If survey finds the backfill is larger or
messier than wave 27's re-parent — a real risk, since `surface` is populated on
24 of 343 rows and is free prose — the wave scopes the guard's population to
rows the backfill actually reached and says so, rather than arming a refusal
over rows it cannot classify.

On the third instrument slice: it is not a third, it is the CORRECT first.
Without the discrimination, both of the lead's options are workarounds, and this
Paper has the run output proving it. The wave's own ratio discipline still binds
— if the discrimination and the environment declaration cannot be shown to be
one coherent slice at Decide, the environment half merges with #9356 and the
wave spends the recovered budget on a fifth person body.

boundary, not a load-bearing leg under today's corpus. An epic whose thesis is
“stop asserting what you did not measure” cannot ship a header comment that does

2. `:nxdomain` IS AN ANSWER, NOT A FAULT (commits 90ab9999a + 438495a96) — a
REVIEW DECISION superseding the letter of D332. The s7 builder built D332 as
written, measured that one fifth of it was wrong, and filed task-
3fbfff8c97b50c8f at priority 0 rather than quietly re-deciding. He was right on
both counts. Re-measured at review on this host: `:inet.getaddrs` of a non-
existent name returns `{:error, :nxdomain}` on BOTH families — which is
precisely what a freshly attached, still-propagating domain looks like through
the real transport. Shipped literally, D332 would have turned the MOST COMMON
waiting state into “we could not check” and stripped its propagation advice: one
lie swapped for another, in the very rung the slice existed to make honest.
NXDOMAIN means the resolver ANSWERED. Fixed with `@answered_empty [:nxdomain]`,
and — the half that matters for the standing test — the DNS test fakes now
RETURN nxdomain for a missing record instead of `{:ok, []}`, a shape production
never produces. task-3fbfff8c97b50c8f is closed with the ruling, its measurement
and its residual gap on the record.

3. THE REAP ALERT CAP IS NOW DRIVEN (commit ab2a6dce5). `@reap_alert_cap 25`
decides that the 26th owner in a cluster incident hears nothing by email — a
person-facing SUPPRESSION that no fixture crossed. The builder flagged it
against himself; the review paid it: a test fails 27 deployments in one sweep

`Registry.reap_alert_cap/0` exposed so the test reads the real constant instead
of a copy. Mutation-proved: replacing `Enum.split(alerts, @reap_alert_cap)` with
`{alerts, []}` reds exactly that test and nothing else.

4. THE MUTATION ANCHORS IN s1's CONTROL (commit 52a80c7be).

left the floor mutation un-applied and the control passing vacuously. Each
anchor is now asserted present before use.
