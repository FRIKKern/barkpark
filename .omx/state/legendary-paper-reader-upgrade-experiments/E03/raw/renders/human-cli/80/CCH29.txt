Direction debate                                                                
                                                                                
Two rivals were developed far enough to be shippable, then argued against each  
other. Neither was a straw man; both are what a reasonable strategist would do  
with this wish.                                                                 
                                                                                
Rival A — SCREENS ONLY                                                          
                                                                                
Spend the whole wave on person rows. Take s2 because it is mandated and nothing 
else instrumental. The argument is strong: the epic’s vision sentence is the    
console stops lying, the ratio floor exists because instrument work keeps       
crowding out screens, and the board carries at least a dozen live person rows.  
The finished wave would be six screens a person can actually reach, which is the
most legible good this epic produces.                                           
                                                                                
Why it loses: it was true yesterday and stopped being true today. The day four  
contexts started blocking, a host nobody in this repo controls acquired the     
power to freeze every merge in the repository — and I measured the coupling live
rather than inferring it (not ok 56). Rival A spends the wave on this epic’s    
screens while every OTHER concurrent wave pays the outage. And it misreads the  
vision: a gate that reds for the wrong reason is the same lie, pointed at the   
fleet instead of at a customer.                                                 
                                                                                
Rival B — THE LANE, END TO END                                                  
                                                                                
An instrument wave. Harden all four required contexts, finish s2, promote D307, 
run the live half of required-checks.test.sh at four contexts for the first     
time, adjudicate Security gate’s registrability now that it is green. The       
argument: the merge lane is the single highest-leverage surface in the repo and 
it has never been exercised in its current shape.                               
                                                                                
Why it loses: it violates two of this epic’s own laws at once. The ratio floor  
demands four person bodies. And Standing Law 0 sends every gate, generator,     
harness and required-checks row to the SUCCESSOR epic cch-instruments-epic — so 
an all-instrument wave filed under this parent would breach the filing law that 
wave 28 just armed a door guard for. Rival B is a real wave; it is the          
successor’s wave, not this one’s.                                               
                                                                                
Why the winner won                                                              
                                                                                
The winner is not a split of A and B. It is the observation that the SAME       
sentence generates both halves, so the wave does not have to choose. "I could   
not look" ≠ "the thing is broken" is what s2 teaches a predicate, what the lane 
slice teaches a required context, and what four screens on the board are already
failing at. A wave with one sentence is a wave whose Decide phase can adjudicate
slices against a criterion instead of a wishlist — and it satisfies the ratio   
floor with rows chosen on merit rather than on quota.                           
                                                                                
The sharpest attack on the winner, and how the wave absorbs it                  
                                                                                
▌ THE GATE SLICE MAY BE A NON-DEFECT, AND THE "FIX" MAY BE A WEAKENING. On a    
▌ dead server the predicate correctly emitted an INFRA FAULT and the test       
▌ correctly redded. That is an HONEST red. A wave that makes it green has       
▌ softened a guard and called it hardening — the exact failure this epic        
▌ exists to catch, committed by the wave that cites the standing test.          
                                                                                
Absorbed, not hedged. The wave does not green the red. The predicate’s exit-2 on
an unreachable server is correct behaviour and is not touched. The defect is    
narrower and different: a context that BLOCKS a merge is asking a question whose
answer lives on a host outside this repository. The remedy is relocation — the  
live assertion moves somewhere it can be honestly red without freezing the lane 
— plus a hermetic control that is just as cruel and still proves the contrast   
the test exists to prove (a live refusal never reaches the ladder; --ladder-only
does). Three wrong fixes are banned by name in the slice brief, because each is 
green by construction under standing clause 4:                                  
                                                                                
1. try/catch around the spawn — the control passes on a dead server AND on a    
   broken predicate.                                                            
2. assert(status === NO_SEAL || status === INFRA) — a disjunction that admits   
   the failure it was written to detect.                                        
3. Deleting the control — the contrast at :1089 IS the slice that shipped in    
   wave 11; removing it retires a real proof to buy a green.
