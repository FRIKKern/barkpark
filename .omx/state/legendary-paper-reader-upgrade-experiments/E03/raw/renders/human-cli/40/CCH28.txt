THE FINDING: wave 27 named the shallow
clone in its own fix's header comment,
and did not close it

seal-predicate.mjs:453 is one line: `try
{ execFileSync('git', ['-C', REPO,
'merge-base', '--is-ancestor', commit,
'origin/main'], …); } catch {
problems.push(`commit ${commit} is not
an ancestor of origin/main`); }`. The
catch is unconditional. Charter D318
enumerated exactly what it swallows —
“not-a-git-repo / no-origin-main /
shallow-clone / unknown-sha” — and wave
27's fix closed the first two and wrote
the other two into a comment.

The wave-27 header comment says it out
loud, verbatim: “the ancestry `catch` at
the top of `verifyCommit` swallows not-
a-git-repo, no origin/main, a shallow
clone and an unknown sha into one
identical claim about the PRODUCT.” The
remedy that follows checks that
`.github/workflows/cloud.yml` exists and
that `git rev-parse --show-toplevel`
resolves to REPO. A depth-1 CI clone
satisfies both.

THE DIRECTION: one disease, two
audiences — the console and its
instrument both stop asserting what they
did not measure

This wave has one sentence and it points
in both directions at once. A system
that could not determine something must
say so. It must not substitute a
plausible guess, a default, or the
negative branch of a two-way test it was
unable to run.

Pointed at the INSTRUMENT, that sentence
resolves the lead's binary by refusing
it. Option (a) alone leaves the
predicate lying in every other shallow
environment — a container, a fork PR, an
agent's `--depth 1`, any future job.
Option (b) alone leaves standing the
fact the lead correctly identified as
bigger than the test: that every seal
run under this workflow is structurally
unable to evaluate clause (b). The
answer that pays both is the
DISCRIMINATION: teach the git legs to
tell rc 1 (“not an ancestor”, an honest
answer about the product) from rc 128 /
missing object / shallow repository (“I
could not look”, an honest answer about
the environment). Once the predicate can
tell those apart, (b) becomes the
correct shape for the control — it
asserts the floor it is actually about —
and (a) becomes a verified environment
declaration rather than a workaround,
because a regression back to depth-1
would then produce an honest INFRA-FAULT
the job can assert on.

Pointed at the PERSON, the same sentence
is already sitting in the ledger as open
rows. A refused connection is reported
as “A network step timed out. Retry
usually fixes this.” — the console did
not classify the failure, so it guessed,
and the guess carries ADVICE. A person
switches on “Deployment failed” alerts
across email and every chat channel, and
the event has zero producers anywhere in
the repository — the console tells them
they are subscribed to something that
cannot arrive. These are not clipping
bugs. They are the epic's founding
vision, in its purest form, and they are
the wave's person bodies.

The direction debate: three candidates,
argued, one committed

Rival A — “Finish the instrument, build
the door” (the wish read literally)

Take the lead's binary, pick one leg,
and spend the rest of the wave on the
D307 door guard as a full multi-slice
build: the `surface` enum, the backfill,
the fence ruling, the refusal, the
mutation proof. Clean, obedient, and it
retires the highest-leverage row in the
epic.

WHY IT LOSES. Three counts. First,
arithmetic: four instrument slices
against a floor of four person bodies
means eight slices minimum, and this
epic's own history says a wave that
spends 4-of-6 on instruments moves the
row count the wrong way (waves 9/10/11:
+11, +16, +7). Second, the door guard's
dependency chain — enum → backfill →
fence ruling → refusal — is SERIAL and
cannot fan out to parallel builders, so
it converts a fan-out wave into a queue.
Third and worst, it accepts the false
binary. Picking (a) or (b) at speed
leaves the predicate lying in every
shallow environment that is not this one
workflow, and this Paper has now proved
live that such an environment exists and
that the epic's own instrument is in it.

Rival B — “Pay Law 0 to zero and drive
at the seal”

orphans is 57 and a=FAIL is the only
clause failing. Spend the wave closing,
forwarding and adjudicating residue —
including the cch-w22-s7 split D325
already ruled — and get the epic within
sight of an honest SEAL.

WHY IT LOSES. It is the epic's stated
disease wearing a productivity costume.
The charter's vision is that the CONSOLE
stops lying; a wave with zero person
bodies fails the ratio outright and
would be the fourth restatement of a law
rather than a payment of it. It also
mistakes the seal for the goal — D318
proved the predicate could SEAL an epic
that does not exist, which means a
falling orphan count was never the thing
worth optimising. HARVESTED ANYWAY: this
Paper quotes the reading with all three
provenance facts, and the cch-w22-s7
adjudication is booked as a Decide-phase
ledger action for the lead, costing zero
builders.
