The direction

The epic's law has walked one rung per wave. A receipt lies when the value it emits does not descend from the write return. A class lies when it does not descend from its substitution (wave 42). A gate lies when its green costs nothing to produce (wave 43). Wave 45's sentence is the one the new instrument earns:

A PRICE LIES WHEN IT DOES NOT DESCEND FROM A METER THE INSTRUMENT ITSELF DROVE - AND A DISPOSITION LIES WHEN ITS EVIDENCE NAMES A RUN THE INSTRUMENT NEVER RE-TAKES.

What the finished wave looks like: ./scripts/pds-door-census.sh still exits 0 on main and still costs seconds - it is a door, and its own 3.32 s row is its own gate cost, so any design that prices it out of its own door is disqualified before it is written. But every number it prints is now either taken by a meter it drove in THIS run, or a cached measurement carrying the content hash of the instrument it prices, so that a changed instrument turns the row PRICE-STALE and reds. A --measure verb takes the measurement and emits the exact ledger row, so re-pricing is one command rather than a hand ritual - the number descends from a meter the instrument drove, and anyone can re-take it. Dispositions whose evidence is a RUN get re-run under an opt-in, threshold-printed tier, so CONTENT-RED cannot survive its own repair. And an orphaned ledger row is an ERROR, because that is the exact shape both of this wave's own slices create.

The direction debate

Three directions were developed far enough to cost something, then argued against each other.

Rival A - pay the debts, period

Do exactly the wish's items 1 through 5: three deferred slices, two owed reviews, the lv_verdict half, the criterion lesson. Do not touch the census - it exits 0, wave 44 declared it done, and reopening it is a fifth wave of arguing about a column. This is the disciplined reading of the brief and it has a real claim on the wave.

WHY IT LOSES: it hands the wave a false floor. Two of its own three slices silently corrupt the instrument, so "pay the debts" manufactures the next wave's finding. And it violates the mandate the user restates every single wave - DERIVE, NEVER TRANSCRIBE, INCLUDING FROM THIS BRIEF - by inheriting "the price column is an instrument now" as a premise instead of running it. I ran it. It is half an instrument, and the half that is prose is the half the column is named after.

Rival B - measure every price, every run

The pure fix: no cache, no ledger, no staleness possible. The census drives the meter for every row on every invocation and the price column is a measurement by construction.

WHY IT LOSES, on the epic's own evidence: D648 proved wall is not a property of a door on a shared host - a fixed workload's wall spanned 5.8x at constant load while its user CPU spanned 27 percent. A re-measuring census becomes the most expensive program in its own denominator: 40.33 s for pds-ledger-census_test.sh alone, plus the receipt census whose expensive arm D646 showed conceals ~140 s minimum from any outer meter. It would also have to price ITSELF - a fixpoint. Worst of all it converts a 4 s door into an ungateable one, killing the census's own THROUGH row, which is the single most self-defeating outcome available to this wave. The honest design is not "always measure" but "never assert a measurement you cannot re-take, and know when it expired."

Rival C - spend the column, gate the cheap doors

Take the census output at face value and do the obvious thing the wish's title demands - SPEND IT. Put every affordable row through the required gate: hetzner offline at 2.15 s, pds-record-parity.test.sh at 4.45 s, pds-scratch-target_test.sh at 8.91 s. Maximum visible progress, and it is exactly what four waves of arguing were FOR.

WHY IT LOSES AS A WHOLE DIRECTION, and why it is absorbed as a part: gating on unverified prices is D633's sin at one remove. pds-scratch-target_test.sh's own shipped row says "Hermeticity on a runner WITHOUT local Postgres is unproven here" - gating it is a day-one red on a foreign runner, the identical trap wave 44 documented for sheet-grid-js.yml. Rival C survives as exactly ONE new door: the one whose price and green I measured myself today.
