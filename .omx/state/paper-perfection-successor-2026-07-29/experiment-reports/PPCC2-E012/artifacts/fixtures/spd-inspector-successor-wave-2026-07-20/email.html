The finding that reopens the option set

D126's arithmetic, and the criterion-2 evidence string on `spd-b39` that repeats it, hold `panel_px` FIXED at its measured 720 at viewport 1024. But 720 is not a constraint — it is a CHOICE the desk is currently making. The committed artifact records the pane rail per row: at 1024 it is `visible_pane_widths_px: [44, 260]`. The desk is spending 260px on a document-list column while it overlays the inspector on the prose. 1024 = 44 + 260 + 720.

The parent epic's own Vision sentence declares the priority order that was never run at this transition, verbatim: 'all squeeze flows outward by explicit priority (list pane compresses first, then rails to 44px strips, then the Document inspector overlays instead of docking)'. At 1024 the list pane has never been asked to compress. The inspector goes straight to the overlay — step three of a ladder whose first step was skipped.

The machinery to run it ALREADY SHIPS. `BarkparkWeb.Studio.PaneBuilder.display_state/4` returns `:full | :strip | :hidden` per pane. In the `narrow` bucket with an editor open, every pane is `:hidden` except the LAST, which is a 44px `:strip` — which is exactly why the artifact reads `[44]` at 900 and below and `[44, 260]` at 1024, where the `standard` branch instead returns `:strip` iff `collapse?/3` else `:full`. The rule that frees the pixels is written, tested and deployed; it is simply not reachable from the `standard` bucket, and nothing in the codebase lets the inspector's own state influence it.

This arithmetic is a HYPOTHESIS, stated so it can fail. Each is a survey and verify assignment below.

The direction

THE INSPECTOR STOPS BORROWING THE READER'S COLUMN, AND THE DESK PAYS FOR IT IN PRIORITY ORDER. The answer to D127's binary is neither horn alone: the reading bar is LAW at every width where the desk can still pay for the inspector out of lower-priority space, and it is SUSPENDED-AND-SAID only where a run proves there was nothing left to sell. A three-tier ladder, each tier chosen by arithmetic rather than by taste.

TIER 1 — `wide` (1280, 1440). UNCHANGED, and this is a hard constraint rather than an omission. The inspector docks, costs 0.000px on the click, `visible_content_px == content_px`, and five of the six MEETs in the entire user-opened table live here. Any slice this wave dispatches must prove wide moved zero pixels, by the runtime rule-deletion diff D102 used, not by assertion.

TIER 2 — `standard` (1024). THE LIST YIELDS AND THE INSPECTOR DOCKS. On the click that opens the inspector, the desk applies the pane rule it already ships one bucket down; the 260px document-list column folds to nothing (its 44px strip stays on screen and clickable, the same distinction `spd-b29` proved is the difference between a collapsed strip and an un-openable panel), the panel takes the width, and the inspector sits BESIDE the prose instead of on top of it. No overlay. No scrim. No mode. The >=55ch bar HOLDS here and is measured, not exempted. This is the wave's headline, and it converts the single sharpest cell in the whole ledger from 37.90ch to a predicted 60.00ch.

TIER 3 — `narrow` and `phone` (900 and below). THE PANEL STOPS BEING A PANEL. Here the arithmetic is genuinely closed and the charter is right: D113 measured that `.editor-panel`'s left edge is 44px at EVERY sub-wide width — the rail has already yielded everything, there is nothing left to sell — and docking is not merely insufficient but WORSE than the overlay at 800 (a 300px dock inside a 756px panel leaves 376px of content against the overlay's measured 396.9px). So the inspector becomes a SUMMONED DESTINATION: it takes the whole panel, the reading column is LEFT rather than dimmed, and the breadcrumb drill that ALREADY SHIPS at `phone` (the artifact records `crumbs.host_present: true`, `visible: true`, `count: 3` at viewport 500, with `pane_columns: 0`) carries the reader back. This is Sanity's shape, MD3's standard-sheet tier, and the parent epic's own vision sentence for phone, extended upward to every width where docking is dead.

THE UNIFYING RULE, and the aesthetic core: NOWHERE does the desk end up with a reader's prose at 55% black under a panel. The dimming is ABOLISHED, not tuned. A mode either shows the document or it does not, and the shipped design's refusal to choose is the defect D127 is really asking about. Pay for the dock where the desk can afford it; leave the document where it cannot.

The pre-registered rule (D127), stated before the successor's table is consulted

This is written now, in a published Paper, before this wave's shape exists and therefore before its table exists. It will be landed as a numbered decision in the charter FILE in the same wave that cites it (D135), because a Paper's claim to have committed something is a claim like any other and this work verifies claims.

RULE, part 1 — WHERE THE BAR APPLIES. In the user-opened state the >=55ch bar APPLIES at every width where a reading column is on screen: `wide` and `standard`. It is measured there by `visible_meets_55ch` (D139), per face, each figure naming its face, its font-size and its probe-derived px/ch, with cross-face division BANNED (D83). A failure there is a failure; no exemption is available at those widths and none will be sought.

RULE, part 2 — WHERE IT DOES NOT APPLY, AND WHAT REPLACES IT. At `narrow` and `phone`, in the user-opened state ONLY, the bar does not apply, because no reading column is on screen to measure. This is NOT the metric being made unmeasurable: the harm this work measured was OCCLUSION of a column the reader was still trying to read, and removing the column removes the reading, not merely the measurement. The exemption is EARNED by three replacement criteria, all of which must pass or the exemption is void and Tier 3 reverts to a defect:

(a) ROUND-TRIP FIDELITY. default -> user-opened -> dismissed returns the reading column BIT-IDENTICAL to its pre-open value, at every width and every forced face, measured by the instrument in one run rather than compared across runs. A destination the reader cannot get back from is worse than an overlay.

(b) THE EXIT IS CHEAP AND PLURAL. Escape, an explicit close control, and a tap outside all dismiss; focus is trapped while summoned and restored to the invoking control on dismiss; `aria-modal` and `aria-expanded` tell the truth in every state. This is `spd-b12-inspector-overlay-dismiss`'s four criteria, and it is the SPINE of any mode claim — a mode you cannot leave is a trap, and the current one cannot be left. Note the structural constraint the survey must resolve: today's scrim is a `::after` pseudo-element with `pointer-events: none`, so it cannot carry a dismiss handler and a real element or a window-level key handler is required.

(c) THE MODE DECLARES ITSELF. No reading text is left half-legible under a scrim in any state at any width — `scrim_covers_content_fully` must be false, or the scrim absent, in every row of the successor's table. If the shipped shape still dims prose anywhere, the exemption does not apply and the cell counts as a failure.

RULE, part 3 — WHAT MAY NOT MOVE. Viewport 640 stays a named, owned shortfall (D100 as superseded by D106: 50.7ch resolved Iowan, 45.9ch forced Georgia — the '~0.2ch / ~5.4ch' figures may never be quoted, they were the user-opened state read as the default one). Viewport 500 / phone stays never-ch-gated. D107's seven desktop widths are FIXED and may not be renegotiated by any slice that consults the table.

The direction debate — the rivals, and why the winner won

RIVAL 1 — MODE BY DECREE. Change no geometry. Rule the user-opened state an exempt mode, write the rule into the charter before looking, fix only the dismissal grammar, ship. It is the cheapest candidate, it is explicitly sanctioned by D127, and it would close this work in one small wave. REFUSED. It accepts the half-state as permanent: the scrim still dims the entire content box at 55% black and the reader still watches 22.00ch of prose go grey on a single click at 1024. A mode-exemption that leaves the document visible-but-illegible under the panel is not a mode — it is exactly the INDETERMINACY the wish names as the defect, and declaring it legal does not resolve it. Worse, it forecloses Tier 2 permanently: it writes an exemption over the one width the desk could actually have paid for, and a later wave inheriting that exemption would have no reason to re-check the arithmetic.

RIVAL 2 — THE NARROW SHEET. Shrink the inspector below 300px until an in-flow dock fits at 1024. REFUSED on D126's own terms AND on content. The ceiling with the panel frozen is 90px resolved Iowan and 32.4px forced Georgia; nothing that answers to 'metadata panel' fits. And the panel is ALREADY too narrow for its contents at 300px — the overlay block in `root.html.heex` ships `.bp-doc-field { flex-wrap: wrap; }` inside it, an authored admission that the label/value rows collide, and the Relations section holds outbound references plus three groups of inbound backlinks that grow without bound. Narrowing an already-cramped panel to buy fractions of a ch, per face, is the threshold-gerrymandering D83 and D103 forbid by name.

RIVAL 3 — DRILL EVERYWHERE. Make the inspector a summoned destination at every non-`wide` width, 1024 included. This is the strongest rival and it very nearly won: one rule instead of two, one shape to build and test, no new dimension on `display_state`, no collision with the epic's 40-cell literal pin, and it satisfies the pre-registered rule cleanly. It lost on a single point: at 1024 it GIVES AWAY a MEET the desk can afford. Throwing the reader out of their document to check a slug, at a width where the arithmetic says the panel and the prose fit side by side with 60.00ch to spare, is precisely the desk failing to reason about the space it has — the charge this whole epic was filed on. Rival 3 is retained as the NAMED FALLBACK, not discarded (see the absorption below).

WHY THE LADDER WON. It is the only candidate derived from the parent epic's OWN declared priority order rather than from a preference. It converts the sharpest measured cell in the ledger into a predicted pass by arithmetic on measured inputs rather than by changing a rule or moving a threshold. And it makes the pre-registered exemption HONEST in a way no rival can: the bar is suspended only at widths where a run can demonstrate the desk had nothing left to sell — which D113 already demonstrated for 900 and below, and which the charter never even asked at 1024.

The sharpest attack on the winner

THE ATTACK. Tier 2 is bought by giving `display_state` a new input, and `display_state/4`'s 40-cell exhaustive table of hardcoded literals is, by D94's own words, 'the epic's one genuinely non-vacuous wide lock' — the only assertion in the repo that catches a regression of `collapse?/3`, proven by fault injection when its neighbouring 'bit-identical' test stayed green and was later retired by conversion. And D94's re-authored criterion 2 names 'the wide/STANDARD `display_state/4` table is unchanged, pinned by literal constants' as clause (a). Viewport 1024 is `standard`. So this wave's headline collides head-on with the lock the epic wrote to protect itself, at exactly the width it protects.

THE SECOND EDGE OF THE SAME ATTACK. The Tier 2 win is bought by making a user-visible document list DISAPPEAR on a metadata click. If that reads as the desk losing the reader's place, then a 22ch gain has been purchased with a navigation regression that no ch figure in any table will ever show — and this work has been overturned before by exactly that shape of blindness (D96: the secondary pane was dead for five waves behind green tests, because every covering test drove the socket and none drove the DOM).

HOW THE WAVE ABSORBS IT. (1) The new dimension lands as a NEW ARITY — an inspector-aware `display_state/5` delegating to an UNTOUCHED `/4` — so the 40-cell literal table stays byte-identical and D94(a) is satisfied by construction rather than by argument, and the new dimension earns its OWN exhaustive literal table, extending the lock instead of widening it. Any shape that requires editing a pinned literal is refused at Decide. (2) The disappearing-list risk becomes a first-class acceptance criterion rather than a hope: round-trip fidelity must restore the PANES and the reading column, the 44px strip must stay on screen and clickable in the yielded state, and the proof must drive a real browser rather than a socket. (3) Both edges are survey assignments below, and if the survey shows `display_state` cannot take a fifth input without disturbing the pin, the wave falls back to Rival 3 at 1024 — the two shapes share the dismissal grammar, the destination surface, the metric rule and all of the instrument work, so the fallback costs this wave ONE SLICE, never its direction. A direction whose fallback is a rival it already argued is a direction that can survive being wrong.
