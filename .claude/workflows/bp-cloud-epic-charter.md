## Vision

**A warrant is only as strong as the venue that can red it.**

A documented invariant that nothing enforces is not merely useless — it is load-bearing for every reader downstream, and a false one in a canonical place produces the exact behaviour it forbids. Three instances landed in three days, each found only because a wave tripped over it: `Tenancy.Auth`'s "It is total" (two waves cited it *and* independently hand-rolled defences against its falsity); `doc-gates.yml`'s `Blocking:` header and twenty-one `(blocking)` step names for a context branch protection does not require (the doctrine page already carried a bullet headed "A NAME THAT SAYS (blocking) AND HAS NO MERGE AUTHORITY AT ALL", and nothing enforced the cure); and `caps.ex`'s "BYTE-IDENTICAL" fork claim.

This epic stops finding them by accident. It sweeps four bounded, machine-derivable strata — merge authority, the `@canonical` index, the authorization/tenancy/atomicity chokepoints, the doctrine surface — states the denominator and the dropped-as-descriptive count out loud, and classifies every harvested claim into **four** cells, not three: ENFORCED (guard named, and the guard proven able to fail), **ENFORCED-ADVISORY** (a guard that structurally cannot stop a merge), UNENFORCED-BUT-TRUE (the tripwire opportunity), FALSE (corrected in the `#12631` house style, or filed with a counterexample when the code is off-fence).

The finished experience: a reader opening a guarantee sentence can tell in one hop what breaks if it is false; every FALSE claim is corrected or filed with an executable counterexample; and the highest-value TRUE-but-unenforced claims carry mutation-proven tripwires **placed where a red can actually stop a merge**. The ledger is the floor and ships even if every ratchet is cut; the tripwires are the ceiling.
