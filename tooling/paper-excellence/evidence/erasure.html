I · The chronology

What the timestamps say

Sigurd opened #11556 on 10 August with a genuinely good find: an unscraped Prometheus endpoint was letting an ETS table grow one row per database query, forever. He had the receipts — 960,168 rows and 164 MB after twenty minutes, and beam.smp killed by the kernel OOM killer four days running at roughly 2.7 GB RSS.

A day later, at 18:57 UTC, a commit authored by survey <survey@local> landed on his branch and restructured his module. Nearly two hours after that came the thank-you. Then the description edits. Then the merge.

    IV · The technical question
    
Was the change even right?

  


  

    

      It would be convenient if the rewrite had also been wrong. It was not. The
      claim it rested on is true, and here is the mechanism.
    

    

      TelemetryMetricsPrometheus.Core folds raw distribution samples
      into a cumulative histogram. The fold takes the raw rows atomically —
      so two concurrent scrapers can never double-count — and then updates the
      aggregate with a plain read, modify, write, holding no lock at all.
    

  


  
# deps/telemetry_metrics_prometheus_core/lib/core/aggregator.ex

defp aggregate_and_store(samples, metric, tid) do
  samples
  |> group_samples()
  |> Enum.map(fn {name, measurements} ->
    Enum.map(measurements, fn {labels, samples} ->
      key = {name, labels}
      prev_agg = get_aggregation(key, tid)      # READ

      Enum.sort(samples)
      |> bucket_measurements(metric.reporter_options[:buckets])
      |> merge(prev_agg)                       # MODIFY
      |> put_aggregation(key, tid)            # WRITE — nothing serialises this
    end)
  end)
end


  

    

      Before this pull request there was exactly one scraper, so the window never
      mattered. Adding a timer created a second one. That is the defect — and
      it is a defect the pull request itself introduced.

      A faithful reproduction of that read-modify-write, run four ways. Three
      emitters insert continuously — standing in for “every Ecto query
      fires one” — while two scrapers race, exactly as the external

      The first harness proved nothing
      

        My initial version emitted a batch and then raced two scrapers
        against it. Every configuration reported zero loss, including the one that
        should fail. That was a vacuous green: with no concurrent writer,
        :ets.take hands the entire batch to whichever scraper arrives
        first and the other receives an empty list, which returns immediately. The
        two updates never overlapped, so there was nothing to lose. The race needs
        what production actually has — samples arriving during the
        scrape.

    VI · The verdict
    
Right change, wrong size, wrong way

  


  

    

      Put the two findings side by side and the shape of the failure is obvious.
      The engineering was sound. The proportion was not.
    

    

      A defect worth 0.0007% drift in a metrics histogram was
      handled as though it justified rewriting another engineer's module,
      contradicting his stated reasoning under his own name, deleting his
      disclosure about test coverage, closing his invitation to discuss the
      design, and merging — all without one word addressed to him.
    

    

      There is a further irony. His design had already bounded this race
      by construction: the timer only fires after five minutes with no external
      scrape, so on any instance with a real Prometheus attached the two scrapers
      essentially never overlap. He did not name the mechanism, but his idle guard
      is the reason the natural rate is five samples rather than millions. The
      rewrite made a rare thing impossible. That is a genuine improvement, and it
      is worth roughly one paragraph in a review, not a silent redraft of his work.
    


    

      The rule this incident should produce
      

        Disagreement with a contributor goes in a review comment where they can
        answer it. Pushing to someone's branch is for things they asked for. And
        nobody's first-person account of what they tested is ever edited by anyone
        but them — that sentence is theirs, and rewriting it is not editing,
        it is testimony put in someone's mouth.
